# #32995 bench and flights, 2026-10-29: the current input on modified R3 boards, and a GPS false alarm

Two RPI_UAVFC boards, a bench board on an ESC and a bench supply, and the
board in Andy's 5 inch iFlight quad. The current input was measured on the
bench under three firmwares and in two flights; the GPS failed to fix in
both flights, was chased as a firmware regression, and turned out to be
the receiver recovering. Real-flight and bench logs are named, never
committed.

## Boards and firmware

| Board | MCU UID prefix | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| Bench | `D3E02752` | TBS 8S 4-in-1 (AM32 target `TBS_8S_4IN1_F421`), bench supply feeding through the ESC's shunt, analog SmartAudio VTX |
| Quad | `99F517D8` | iFlight 5 inch, iFlight BLITZ E55 4-in-1 (BLHeli_32), u-blox M10 (`ROM SPG 5.10`) on UART1, ELRS on PIOUART0, digital VTX on SERIAL1, switched off for both flights |

| Firmware | What it is | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `f83a2e79` | release branch, build F of 2026-10-28 | bench VTX sweep and temperature run (log `00000052.BIN`) |
| Betaflight `81e82a62a` | `RASPBERRY_PI_UAVFC` target from Betaflight master, 2026-10-28 | bench pin readback over SWD, static load |
| `1f8685e9` | release branch `ArduPilot-4.8-rp2350`, carrying #32995 at `e45dae01b1` | bench static load in ArduPilot, flights 169 and 170, the GPS runs |
| `9347a457` | the 2026-10-03 build `arducopter-gyro-fir-off.apj` | one GPS run (log `00000171.BIN`) and the indoor GPS A/B |

**The boards are not R4.** The 2026-10-28 note calls the bench board R4,
which was Andy's description. Raspberry Pi wrote on 2026-10-29: "the boards
you have are R3s modified with [the current input's pull-down] changed to
10k, but the leaky diode [the clamp on that input] is still fitted, so
you'll see an offset on the current reading. Proper R4 boards are due in a
few days." Both boards here are in that state.

What R4 changes on the current input, restated from Raspberry Pi's
documents without their references: the pull-down from the pin to ground
goes from 82.5k (R3, as `rp2350/DEVELOPMENT.md` describes it) to 10k, and
the Schottky clamps from the battery and current inputs to 3V3 become one
low-leakage dual Schottky. The 120 ohm series resistor and 100 nF at the
pin are unchanged.

The short ESC cable supplied with the board connects only one of the
flight controller connector's two ground pins and one of its two battery
pins, so the whole board's supply current returns through one thin wire.

## 1. Bench, TBS ESC

### The VTX cannot measure the sensor (`f83a2e79`, log `00000052.BIN`)

Stepping the VTX through pit mode, 25, 200, 500 and 800 mW moved the
current pin the wrong way: down by at least 16 mV from pit mode to 800 mW,
where it sat at the ADC's 0 V floor. The battery reading fell 0.12-0.14 V
at 800 mW (11-13 mV at its pin). Dropping from 800 mW (0.68 A on the
supply's meter after warming) to pit mode (0.2 A) raised the current pin
by at least 24 mV and the battery pin by 23 mV at the same moment.

Mechanism, inferred and not measured directly: the VTX current returns
through the flight controller's single ground wire, lifting its ground
against the ESC's, and the ESC's signal is referenced to the ESC's ground.
That makes at least 50-80 mV per amp of board current on this bench,
against a sensor of about 5 mV per amp.

### Temperature drift: the leakage is much smaller on these boards

Cooling at a steady 0.2 A load in pit mode, same log:

| | Old boards (`rp2350/DEVELOPMENT.md`) | Modified R3, bench |
|---|---|---|
| Current pin drift against `MCU.MTemp` | +28.4 mV/degC, R2 0.996, 32-45 degC | +1.35 mV/degC, R2 0.99, 34.4-40.7 degC |
| Battery pin drift, same window | not recorded | +1.70 mV/degC |
| Standing offset, motors stopped | 0.5-0.6 V | 14.5 mV at 0.2 A, 34.6 degC |

The battery divider is low impedance, so leakage into it cannot make 1.70
mV/degC; whatever drives that is common to the ADC inputs. The current
pin's residual drift therefore cannot be pinned on the clamp from this run
(inference).

Disarmed logging at this board's `LOG_BITMASK` wrote 102 MB in 11
minutes, which took over 10 minutes to fetch over MAVLink FTP; copying the
card was much quicker.

### Betaflight reads the same voltage

Betaflight `81e82a62a`, its raw ADC buffer read over SWD without halting
(both cores still running afterwards):

| | Betaflight | ArduPilot, same board, VTX in pit mode |
|---|---|---|
| Current pin (GPIO47) | 25 counts, about 20 mV | 14.5-17.7 mV |
| Battery | 23.4 V | 23.5-23.6 V |

Betaflight's 3.2 A was its current scale of 50 (5 mV/A) applied to that
offset. Raspberry Pi's recommended Betaflight offset of 3000 is 15 mV at
that scale, the same offset.

**A Betaflight finding to report upstream.** Over SWD, Betaflight leaves
GPIO40, 46 and 47 as SIO inputs (function select 5, input buffer on,
output disable off; pad register 0x52), not in the analog state its own
`adc_gpio_init()` sets, so something after that call re-initialises them.
An input buffer left on over an analog pad is the RP2350 E9 leakage
condition. It is not the cause here, since both firmwares read the same
voltage. ArduPilot's `adcRPGpioInit()` sets the analog state in source;
its registers were not read on hardware.

### A static 5 A load: the TBS signal arrives at full scale

A battery discharger as a constant 5.00 A load, soldered downstream of
the ESC's shunt with its return to the ESC's battery pad, so none of it
passes the flight controller's ground. The discharger ramps up over about
15 s and pauses for about 0.3 s every 5.3 s; both are excluded.

Betaflight, pin read over SWD at 10 Hz:

| | Pin |
|---|---|
| Load off | 17.30 mV |
| 5.00 A, pauses excluded (362 samples) | 42.43 mV |
| Step | 25.13 mV, **5.03 mV/A** |

ArduPilot `1f8685e9`, `BATT_AMP_PERVLT` 200, `BATT_AMP_OFFSET` 0.017,
`SYS_STATUS` current at 10 Hz:

| Period | Reading | Step from load off |
|---|---|---|
| Load off (2095 samples) | -0.11 A, sd 0.39 | |
| Load 1, 40 s | 4.86 A, sd 0.04 | 4.97 A, 4.97 mV/A |
| Load 2, 39 s | 4.98 A, sd 0.16 | 5.09 A, 5.09 mV/A |

TBS documents 5 mV/A, so the full signal reaches the pin through the 10k
pull-down: this ESC drives the line from a low impedance. AM32's generic
default of 20 mV/A (the target sets no scale) is wrong for it. Both
firmwares agree.

For a TBS ESC on these boards: `BATT_AMP_PERVLT` 200, and
`BATT_AMP_OFFSET` about 0.0165 V, which centres the zero at the bench's
temperature but moves 1.35 mV/degC (0.27 A/degC) with it. One ADC step
(0.8 mV) is 0.16 A at this scale.

## 2. Flights on the quad, iFlight BLITZ E55, `1f8685e9`

Charger figures are what Andy's charger put back after each flight.

### Log `00000169.BIN`: no tracking

`BATT_AMP_PERVLT` 10, `BATT_AMP_OFFSET` 0, 616 s log, about 60 s airborne.

| Segment | Reading | Pin | Battery | `MCU.MTemp` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disarmed, before arming | 0.68 A | 68 mV | 25.53 V | 26.2 degC |
| Armed, idle | 0.68 A | 69 mV | 25.52 V | 26.2 degC |
| Airborne | 0.64 A (0.45-0.79) | 64 mV | 24.85 V | 21.5 degC |
| Disarmed, after landing | 0.49 A | 49 mV | 25.03 V | 21.5 degC |

The battery sagged 0.7 V with motor RPM (r -0.49), but the reading moved
slightly against RPM (r -0.24) and with battery voltage (r +0.93). Logged
104 mAh against 172 mAh from the charger.

### Log `00000170.BIN` (copied as `log170.bin`): weak tracking

`BATT_AMP_PERVLT` 50 (iFlight's listed 20 mV/A), `BATT_AMP_OFFSET`
0.0165, about 77 s airborne.

| Segment | Reading | Pin | Battery | `MCU.MTemp` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disarmed, before arming | 2.50 A | 66.6 mV | 25.55 V | 25.7 degC |
| Armed, idle | 2.56 A | 67.8 mV | 25.54 V | 25.8 degC |
| Airborne | 2.37 A mean (1.27-5.21) | 42-121 mV | 24.76 V | 21.1 degC |
| Disarmed, after landing | 1.53 A | 47.2 mV | 24.92 V | 21.1 degC |

The reading rose with motor load this time (r 0.59), peaking in the
hardest climbs. Logged 168 mAh against 196 mAh from the charger.

### What reached the pin (estimates)

- **Gain.** The biggest climb raised the pin about 60 mV above hover for
  about 15 A more, by the eRPM current model in `rp2350/DEVELOPMENT.md`
  (fitted on the Evoque F5): about 3 mV/A. From the charger, splitting
  196 mAh into about 27 mAh disarmed (196 s at about 0.5 A) and the rest
  airborne gives about 7 A mean in the air, against which the pin rose
  only 12-17 mV above a zero that was itself moving: about 2 mV/A, +/-50%.
  Either way about a tenth to a sixth of iFlight's listed 20 mV/A, which
  is a retailer listing, not verified for this ESC.
- **Zero.** 67 mV before arming to 47 mV after landing while the die
  cooled 4.6 degC: about 4 mV/degC, three times the bench's TBS figure,
  and at 2-3 mV/A as large as a whole hover.
- **Why the logged mAh looked close.** About 120 mAh was logged
  disarmed, because the 16.5 mV offset left a 47-67 mV zero unsubtracted,
  and about 54 mAh airborne against about 170 true. The errors cancelled.

The quad's analog current is not usable with this ESC on these boards:
the zero moves as much as the signal.

### Hypothesis: a high source impedance divided by the 10k pull-down

Not measured. If the E55 drives its current line through tens of kohm,
the 10k pull-down divides its signal, and the clamp's leakage, with
nothing stiff holding the node, sets the zero. The pieces fit without
proving it:

- the old boards' 0.5-0.6 V across 82.5k is about 7 uA; 7 uA into 10k is
  about 70 mV, the quad's disarmed zero;
- the old boards' +28.4 mV/degC scaled by 10k/82.5k is about 3.4
  mV/degC, against the quad's 4;
- the TBS ESC, which drives the line stiffly (full 5.03 mV/A through the
  10k), shows a 17 mV zero drifting 1.35 mV/degC on the bench board;
- if the quad is the Evoque F5 of `rp2350/DEVELOPMENT.md` (not
  confirmed) and the E55 really puts out 20 mV/A, its 14.6 mV/A on an
  82.5k board implies a source of about 30k, and 2-3 mV/A on 10k implies
  about 60-90k. The same order, not a match; that airframe's gain also
  moved 26% between two flights.

If it holds, R4 does not fix this ESC: it keeps the 10k pull-down, and its
low-leakage clamp would allow a larger one again. That goes back to
Raspberry Pi once measured.

The test Andy has the instructions for, quad on its pack, disarmed:
1. flight controller alone on USB, ESC cable unplugged: the current pin
   against ground (or the reading in ArduPilot). About 60-70 mV would put
   the zero on the board's clamp;
2. ESC powered, flight controller unplugged: pin 5 at the loose end of
   the cable (CUR) against the battery's balance-lead negative, `V0`;
   then 10k from a 1.5 V cell (`Vcell`) into the same pin, `V1`. Source
   resistance `Rs = 10k x (V1 - V0) / (Vcell - V1)`. About 15 mV for
   `V1` means an amplifier output of about 100 ohm and the hypothesis
   is wrong; about 1.4 V means about 90k and it holds. Never probe pins
   2 (pack voltage) and 3 (ground) of the loose plug.

### Withdrawn on 2026-10-29

Kept so they do not come back:

- **"The offset comes from the ESC, not the board"** (13:37, from the R4
  drawing, reasoning that R4's clamp cannot leak 1.5 uA). Wrong board:
  these are modified R3s with the leaky clamp, per Raspberry Pi.
- **"Ground shift from the digital VTX powering up on arming hid the
  hover signal"** (15:18). The VTX was off for the flight (Andy). The
  ground shift itself is measured on the bench (section 1); it just was
  not acting in flight 169.
- **"The ESC's signal is not reaching the pin"** (log 169). Log 170
  shows it does reach it, weakly.

## 3. Flight performance at `e45dae01b1` on hardware (log `00000169.BIN`)

Armed about 70 s: rate loop 1600 Hz, worst gap (`RTDT`) 0.97 ms, against
under 1.7 ms in earlier flights; main loop 199 Hz with no long loops, load
about 50%; vibration 9-12 m/s/s mean, 21 peak, no clipping; bidirectional
DShot RPM on all four motors with about 0% errors; no log data dropped.

The same board disarmed on the ground beforehand (`@SYS/threads.txt`,
`tasks.txt`, `uarts.txt`): rate loop 1612 Hz, core0 72.8% (peak 77.8%),
core1 50.9%, against 1604-1611 Hz, 68-75% and 47-48% recorded before. ELRS 333 Hz on
PIOUART0 and the GPS at 230400 on UART1 with no new errors in a steady
window; the earlier counts (630 framing errors and 2 overruns on ELRS, 362
dropped bytes on the GPS port) all dated from start-up and baud probing.
`AP_ESC_Telem::update` showed 436 scheduler overruns (worst 173 us) and
`GCS::update_send` 97; there is no earlier figure to compare.

Every `SERIALn_OPTIONS` on the quad is 0, so no port runs half duplex:
commit 48's echo window (half duplex only) did not run in these flights
and is still unflown.

## 4. GPS: a false alarm

### Symptom

Flights 169 and 170 on `1f8685e9`, outdoors under cloud: the M10 was
detected at 230400 and streamed at 5 Hz with no gaps (`GPA.Delta` 200 ms
throughout), but never fixed. Log 169: no time at boot (time of week
counting from receiver start), no position in 10 minutes. Log 170: no time
at boot, time of week decoded by about 53 s and the week by about 94 s,
then from about 234 s a nonsense position (48.8N 40.9E, about 12 km up,
moving about 300 m/s), no fix in 4.5 minutes. The GPS parameters and the
detection messages matched `log159.bin` (2026-10-04, board
`53165524`, `9347a457`), which had a fix from the start. Of 80 earlier
logs from this quad, 32 had a 3D fix (10-29 satellites) and 19 flew
Loiter, on three earlier boards.

### Old firmware on the same board

Andy flashed `9347a457` (board ID 7160 against today's 7180, so
`uploader.py --force`; the RPI_UAVFC bootloader does not check the app)
and ran it outdoors at 16:53, log `00000171.BIN`. Time of week known at
boot, week by 33 s, nonsense positions from 43 to 74 s (about 43.9N 37.0W,
mid-Atlantic, 10 km up), then a 3D fix at 84 s with 6-8 satellites and
HDOP 2.1-3.2. At the time this read as "the old firmware works".

### Indoor A/B through the passthrough

Same board and spot indoors, battery on, disarmed, 150 s each, the
receiver polled once a second through `SERIAL_PASS1` 0 / `SERIAL_PASS2` 2
(`tools/gpsrf.py`). ArduPilot's GPS driver is silent during passthrough,
so this tests the board's RF environment, not the driver.

| | `9347a457` | `1f8685e9` |
|---|---|---|
| MON-RF jamming state | 0 (monitor off) | 0 |
| MON-RF CW suppression (0-255) | median 7, max 9 | median 7, max 10 |
| MON-RF noise per ms | median 92 | median 93 |
| MON-RF AGC | median 2046 | median 2046 |
| MON-SPAN, 32 MHz around 1579.5 MHz | flat, peaks +4 to +8 dB anywhere, L1 bin +1 to +3 dB | same |
| NAV-SAT best C/N0 | 27 dB-Hz (1-2 satellites, indoors) | 26 dB-Hz |

No difference, and no spur at 1575.000 MHz.

### Resolution

At 17:47, `1f8685e9` again, on a fresh SD card (log `00000001.BIN` on that
card): 3D fix from the first GPS record at 13 s, 9-11 satellites, HDOP
1.32-1.64 (one "High GPS HDOP" pre-arm at 69 s). The receiver knew the
time at boot, so it was a warm start. Andy reports quick locks since.

Conclusion: not the firmware. The receiver recovered over successive
power-ups (169 nothing, 170 a bad position, 171 a weak fix, then a quick
one), and the firmware swap landed in the middle. The cause of the slow
start is not established. Andy's suggestion: the quad was last used in
Canada, and a receiver seeded with a position, almanac and ephemeris
from another continent predicts the wrong satellites and Doppler until it
gives up and searches cold. The mid-Atlantic position in log 171 fits
that; log 170's southern Russia does not. Hypothesis only.

The old-board logs (14-17 satellites, HDOP about 0.8) were warm starts,
on other days, about 2.3 km away; they are not comparable with these.

### Withdrawn on the way

- **"The new board, not the firmware"** (16:10, from comparing logs 160
  and 169 across days and places).
- **"It is the firmware"** (after the 16:53 run on `9347a457`), resting
  on one run that was the third power-up of the recovery.
- **"The 225 MHz clock's 7th harmonic (1575.000 MHz) jams L1"** (16:06).
  Every clock derived from 225 MHz has a harmonic there (flash at 75 MHz,
  the SD card at 22.5 MHz), but indoors the spectrum is flat on both
  builds and GPS worked on the earlier boards at the same clock. Not
  tested outdoors.

### The firmware diff, by inspection

Derived from the source, not measured. Between `9347a457` and
`1f8685e9` the clock tree is identical (225 MHz system, QMI divider 3 so
75 MHz flash, SD card at 22.5 MHz high speed in both; only the SD init
clock moved, 439 to 399 kHz), `AP_GPS_UBLOX.cpp` is unchanged, the ChibiOS
submodule is the same commit, and the UART driver changes touch USB only.

## 5. Gotchas found on the way

- **Serial passthrough leaves MAVLink on USB deaf until a reboot**
  (upstream ArduPilot, observed on `9347a457`, same code at `1f8685e9` by
  inspection). `GCS::passthru_timer()` calls `port1->begin()` from the
  timer thread, `UARTDriver::_begin()` makes the calling thread the
  port's owner, and `_available()`/`_read()` return nothing to any other
  thread. After the passthrough times out the board keeps sending
  heartbeats but ignores every request, reboot included. Worth an
  upstream issue.
- **With USB as `SERIAL_PASS1`, the PC's baud sets the GPS port's baud**
  (`get_usb_baud()`), not `SERIAL2_BAUD`. Open the USB port at the GPS's
  own rate (230400 here) or the receiver is heard as noise.
- **`LOG_DISARMED` 2 latches USB for the whole boot.**
  `GPIO::set_usb_connected()` is set on first USB activity and never
  cleared, so a board that booted on USB never logs disarmed, even after
  USB is unplugged. One outdoor GPS run was lost that way. Use
  `LOG_DISARMED` 1 for a test that starts on USB.
- **UBX can be sent to the receiver through ArduPilot** as
  `GPS_RTCM_DATA` from one MAVLink link (`tools/gpsinject.py`): a MON-VER
  poll comes back as ArduPilot's "u-blox 1 HW ... SW ..." text, and a
  RAM-layer `CFG-VALSET` of `CFG-MSGOUT-UBX_MON_RF_UART1` makes ArduPilot
  log MON-RF as `UBX1`. The setting lasts until the receiver loses power.
- **`tools/pinlog.py`'s buffer address is per Betaflight build**: 0x20057778
  is `adcValues` in the `81e82a62a` build flashed on 2026-10-29.

## 6. The quad's old SD card

At 12:22 Windows reported errors on the card after log 169 (that log
parsed without a bad byte). That evening the flight controller's
directory listing showed three entries the logger did not list, `0000078B.IN`
(2985984 bytes), `0000081B.IN` (6230016) and `0000088B.IN` (1183744): the
names read like `00000078.BIN` and so on, shifted one byte, but the sizes
match none of the saved copies of logs 78, 81 and 88. By evening Windows
could not see the card at all; Andy has since formatted it, so nothing is
left to examine. Not established whether it was power removed with a log
open (`LOG_DISARMED` 2 keeps one open), a failing card, or the SD path,
which has changed since `9347a457` (32 KB writes applied at mount, slower
init clock). Watch the new card's listings for mangled names.
