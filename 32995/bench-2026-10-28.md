# #32995 bench, 2026-10-28: SmartAudio and the half-duplex echo window

RPI_UAVFC R4 (Andy's description of the board; board ID 7180) on USB, with
an analog SmartAudio VTX on the VID connector and its antenna fitted.
SERIAL4 is PIOUART1, half duplex on GPIO16. The board was found configured
for it: `SERIAL4_PROTOCOL` 37, `SERIAL4_OPTIONS` 4, `VTX_ENABLE` 1,
`VTX_OPTIONS` 17 (pit mode and `VTX_PULLDOWN`), `VTX_BAND` 3,
`VTX_CHANNEL` 0 (F1, 5740 MHz), `VTX_POWER` 25, `OSD_TYPE` 6. MAVLink and
`@SYS/uarts.txt` were read over USB with pymavlink; no SWD.

The two commits measured here were pushed as `7873a96aa0` and
`9a9765e128`, then moved unchanged (same patch-ids) to 48 and 49 of
`d5cb7ecb59` as `426f25a3bb` and `afd80cc619`, and are 48 and 49 of
`e45dae01b1` as `2f880ec7b4` and `854679d856`, again unchanged. The
numbers below are on the code states named in "Builds", not on either
later head.

The VTX is SmartAudio v2, as Andy confirmed on 2026-10-28; the version
was not decoded from the wire here. Its settings reply is 11 bytes.

## The finding under test

AP-Review, from its 2026-10-23 review on: half-duplex echo suppression on a
PIO UART ended only when the caller next called `available()` or
`flush()`, so a reply arriving before that was dropped with the echo.
SmartAudio flushes straight after writing and hides it; FPort, SRXL2 and
AP_IBus_Telem do not flush.

MAVLink SERIAL_CONTROL cannot show it: it polls `available()` every 1 ms
after writing, which closed the old window within about a millisecond of
the last stop bit, sooner than a VTX replies (derived from the source, not
measured). Build A below shows only that this VTX's reply starts within
150 ms of the request.

At boot, every build from D to G sent one request and took one 11-byte
reply. Three runs in fifteen had one SET that took 6.8 s, retried after

the change. The IRQ maximum varies from 6 to 27 us within one build and
includes preemption; it does not separate D from the fixed builds. The
PIO1 interrupt load rises a few us/s with the fix (one extra interrupt per
transmission).

F gave TX 66 then 222, RX 0: the battery input read 0.47 V and the VTX
had lost power while hardware was being re-plugged. With 16.5 V back, G
on the same image passed. Not a firmware result.

`uarts.txt`'s framing-error count on SERIAL4 rises by roughly 3 to 12 per
exchange on every build, D included. It fits the line resting low on the
pull-down after each turnaround (mechanism only, not measured).

## 3. An upload that probed the Pico 2

Between about 17:24 and 17:28 UTC the first upload of F was given every
port from COM3 to COM40 but COM10. It found the Pico 2's ArduPilot
bootloader waiting on COM16 (board 7181), refused it as the wrong board
("Firmware not suitable"), and kept retrying on that port until it gave
up; it never reached RPI_UAVFC. Nothing was erased or written on the Pico
2, but the port was held and probed in that window. The Pico2 session's
unexplained boot with internal error 0x8000 after two back-to-back uploads
may or may not overlap it; the times were not compared. Uploads to
RPI_UAVFC after that were given COM15 alone.

## 4. Review of the fix, and what was rejected

A quick /pr-review on the two commits (mechanical gate, the bot's thread,
Codex cold reads of the new commits, Codex audits of the findings). All
findings below are derived from the source, not provoked on the board.

Accepted and fixed before the push:

- Cold read of `01c1fb3db8`: `_write()` set `_hd_echo_active` outside the
  port lock. On two cores the interrupt could see an empty ring and FIFO,
  the writer then queue bytes and set the flag, and the interrupt then
  clear it, so that transmission's echo reached the reader. The interrupt's
  own drain could also send newly queued bytes before the flag was set.
  The window now opens in `_drain_tx_fifo()`, the only writer of TXF,
  under the same lock the interrupt closes it under, and the ring check in
  `_hd_echo_check()` is gone. Setting the flag under the lock had been
  considered while writing the fix and backed out on the grounds that the
  ring check covered it; it did not on two cores.
- Cold read of the result (`64ae824d98`): `_hd_echo_check()` read the TX
  state machine's PC before FSTAT, so a byte pulled between the two reads
  looked idle. FSTAT is now read first: nothing refills the FIFO while the
  lock is held, so a byte it held is in the shift register by the time the
  PC is read.

Rejected, with the reason:

- Audit of the race fix, PARTIAL: a reply that reaches the FIFO while the
  interrupt itself is held off past the far end's turnaround is purged
  with the echo. Once echo and reply are both in the FIFO nothing tells
  them apart, so no ordering fixes it; it needs the interrupt delayed by
  milliseconds, against the caller-poll delay the original finding was
  about. Documented in the `_hd_echo_check()` comment and DEVELOPMENT.md
  instead of changed.
- Cold read: the purge loop `01c1fb3db8` adds is unbounded. True of that
  commit alone; the next commit bounds it with the other four.
- A timeout on the port lock's spin, asked about when the loop bounds went
  in so that no hang could reach the watchdog. Not added: giving up leaves
  the level-triggered source raised, so the interrupt re-enters at once;
  carrying on without the lock corrupts the ring; and a false trigger
  would cut a port such as RC input. Every holder is a short fixed section
  with kernel interrupts masked, which neither a context switch nor the
  core1 flash-write park (doorbell at the lowest priority) can interrupt,
  so the spin is bounded once the loops inside those sections are.

Audit of the bot's own finding at `8eabf4f1ea`: CLOSED.

STM32: MatekH743 copter with `--consistent-builds` was byte-identical with
and without the commits at `01c1fb3db8` and at `8eabf4f1ea`. It was not
re-run after the two race fixes, which changed only `PIOUART.cpp` (built
only with `HAL_HAVE_PIO_UARTS`) and DEVELOPMENT.md; the Pico2 session's
squash 8 measured `d5cb7ecb59` itself.

## 5. What was not exercised

  provoked on the board.
