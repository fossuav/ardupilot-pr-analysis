# #32995 release-testing flights, 2026-11-23: the alpha2 flight card

Three logs, one flight each, from Andy's quad (board `99F517D8`, a
modified R3; iFlight BLITZ E55; u-blox M10; ELRS; MSP DisplayPort OSD; four
NeoPixels), flown to `Tools/RP2350/flight-test/FLIGHT_CARD.md` on
`ArduPilot-4.8-rp2350-alpha2` at `031f2549`. That build is #32995 at
`5e97be2505`, the follow-up PRs, the release branch's commits and six
flight-test commits, on ChibiOS `f92aa3d2`.

The card asks how busy each core is in flight, what LED_roll costs, and
whether the right code is in SRAM.

The port was clean on all three flights: no failsafe, internal error, long
loop, log drop or ESC telemetry error. **The card's hard-flying leg was not
flown**, so every load figure here is for low-throttle flight. Core1 runs
at 64-65% armed against 45% after disarm. LED_roll costs core0 5.8 points
and core1 0.4. Core1's placement is nearly right, with one real miss
(`__ieee754_rem_pio2f`). Core0's profile is too incomplete to judge its
placement.

Logs are named as on the quad's card and never committed. `INS_ACC_ID`
4456706 as before, Replay records present. Times are seconds from the first
message of each log.

### Core1

Core1's flash time is almost all two functions:

- `__ieee754_rem_pio2f`, 4.44% of busy, 620 B. The registry leaves it out
  because it "did not profile". In flight it is core1's hottest flash
  function. `NotchFilter::init_with_A_and_Q()` takes `sinf` and `cosf` of
  `2*pi*f/fs`, and newlib reduces the range above pi/4, which at the 3201
  Hz gyro rate is 400 Hz. With the motors at a median 178-180 Hz, harmonics
  3 and 4 are above that in 97-99% of samples, harmonic 2 in 7-10% and
  harmonic 1 never (`ft_notch_pio2.py`). At the 40 Hz base frequency the
  highest centre is 160 Hz, so a bench with stopped motors would not reach
  this path (derived from the source, not run).
- `__udivmoddi4`, 2.13% of busy, 748 B. The disassembly shows three placed
  functions calling the 64-bit divide: `AP_DAL::start_frame` and
  `NavEKF3_core::readIMUData`, which are core0 code, and
  `sysIdleEnterMeasure`, twice per idle entry for its
  `cumulative / n` averages. So on core1 it is largely the cost of
  `BRD_IDLE_STATS` 1, the instrument the card turned on (derived from the
  disassembly, not A/B tested). The registry says this symbol must stay in
  flash because early boot calls it.

| Purpose | Share | Of which in flash |
|---|---|---|
| Notch coefficient update, with its `sinf` and `cosf` | 23.60% | 4.44% |
| Notch apply | 16.43% | 0 |
| Kernel | 9.53% | 0 |
| Motors and servo output | 9.22% | 0 |
| DShot | 8.21% | 0 |
| ESC RPM for the notch | 2.44% | 0 |
| 64-bit divide | 2.36% | 2.26% |
| Attitude control | 1.45% | 0.24% |

The coefficient update is about 15.6 points of core1's load. The quad has
`INS_HNTCH_OPTS` 22, which includes `LoopRateUpdate`, so the rate thread
re-centres every notch at half the gyro rate. On the bench, clearing that
option took armed core1 from 66% to 56.5% and the update chain from about
10% to 1.4% of core1 ([../34436/README.md](../34436/README.md)). That
record still owes a flight comparing notch attenuation with the option off.

### Core0

Core0 cannot be judged from this profile. Its table filled, dropping 18.3%
of busy samples, and the dump lists 71.8% against the 81% the card gives
for the bench. What it does show is a flat flash profile: no flash function
is above 0.71% of busy (`__udivmoddi4`, then `shape_pos_vel_accel` 0.57%
and `GCS::update_sensor_status_flags` 0.53%). Veneers, the flash stubs that
reach SRAM, are 3.89%.

A placed function missing from the list is not cold. 642 of the 1,078
placed functions (60.9 of 110.7 KB) have no listed sample, and they include
`NavEKF3_core::CovariancePrediction`, which runs continuously. At 19,126 B
it spans about 1,195 16-byte lines, so with a list floor of 39 samples a
line it could hold up to about 45,000 samples, 14% of core0's busy time,
without one line being listed.

## 5. Other findings

- OSD stack: 96 B free of 1504 on flights 1 and 2 and 88 B on flight 3,
  against 104 B in the logs of 2026-11-07. This is the smallest margin of
  any thread. PR #34623's static analysis put this thread over budget
  (2026-11-19 section of the README).
- The 2026-11-19 stack trims held: rate 2,480 B free of 3,296, `c1_main`
  536 B of 640 in `threads.txt`.
- `STAK.Total` for `c1_main` is wrong: 10,928 B in logs 12 and 13 and 6,880
  B in log 14, against 640 B. `log_stack_info()` takes the size as the
  distance from the stack base to the thread context, and core1's main
  thread keeps its context in its OS instance (derived from the source).
  `threads.txt` has the fix and the log path does not. `Free` is
  unaffected.
- "GPS Glitch or Compass error" in flight in log 12 (538.2-552.8 s) and log
  14 (144.4-149.6 s), and on the ground in logs 12 and 13. `XKF4.FS` stayed
  0. Log 12 flew on 4-6 satellites with HDop up to 62.5, logs 13 and 14 on
  9-12. `GPA.SAcc` peaked at 2.5, 3.2 and 3.5 m/s. The EKF origin took
  369.7 s in log 12, 17.5 s in log 13 and 99.8 s in log 14.
- UART receive drops: one row on SERIAL3 (CRSF), 74-79 B/s about 1 s after
  boot in each log, none in flight. Logs 8 and 9 also drop bytes at boot.
  Not a regression.
- `RSSI.RXLQ` in flight 96-100, `VIBE` no clipping, peak 63.6 m/s/s.

## Tools

- `ft_card.py` - the card's comparison per log: identity, events, messages,
  load, rate loop, memory, logging, ESC, stacks and the `LEDC` windows.
- `ft_card_detail.py` - flight intensity, UART drops per port and GPS
  quality.
- `ft_intensity.py` - throttle, current, speed and UART drops of an older

  registries.

## 15. The load peak after a start

`PEAK` in `threads.txt` is highest in the first read after the measurement
starts, on either core, and is not load:

- Log 12's boot, read on the ground: core1 97.1%, with a highest 1 Hz
  `RPLD.C1` of 65.6%.
- Log 15, the copy taken at the second arming: core0 93.6%, with a highest
  1 Hz `RPLD.C0` of 57.4%. The next copy reads 52.4% and 56.9%.

`sysIdleEnterMeasure()` computes the load from the mean run and idle
periods. The first values come from one of each, in whole microseconds on
RP2350, and the highest stays in the peak until it is next cleared. An idle
period under 1 us would read as 100% and pass Copter's 99.5% takeoff limit
(derived from the source, not seen). `BRD_IDLE_STATS` 1 is the RPI_UAVFC
default.

The fix lets the average set a peak only once it holds 0.1 s of run and
idle time. A/B over 24 disarmed boots, flashed A C C A with six boots each,
reading `threads.txt` to start the measurement, again 4 s later, and again
4 s after that as a control. A is `031f2549`. C is `fa141ffb`, the same
logic in alpha2's per-core `sysperf.c`, a bench-only commit on no branch.

| Control read, core0 `PEAK` | 33.9-34.4% | 34.1-34.5% |
| Control read, core1 `PEAK` | 49.5-50.0% | 50.0% |

Load in the same windows is 33-34% on core0 and 49.5-50.0% on core1.

## 16. The notch change as its own PR, and flash against arithmetic

Andy asked for the notch change to be its own PR rather than part of
#32995, explained in the code, referenced, with its inaccuracy documented.
It is on local branch `pr-notch-sinf-cosf`, on master `5ec9286ba1`, not
pushed: `0d9b8c0005` adds the coefficient test and `c8441d894f` adds
`calculate_sinf_cosf()`. `pr-notch-sin-cos` is the same change under its
earlier name and is superseded.

The polynomials are now Cephes' published `sinf.c` constants, checked
against the netlib source, in place of `fit_coeffs.py`'s fit. The two are
equally accurate. Every float angle from 1e-4 to pi, with and without fused
multiply-add (`tools/notch_sincos/accuracy_source.cpp`, on the source text):

| | Max error | ulp |
|---|---|---|
| Helper sine | 7.0e-8 | 1.4 |
| Helper cosine | 7.8e-8 | 1.5 |
| glibc `sinf()`, `cosf()` | 3.3e-8 | 0.6 |

Against the same notch with libm, about 90% of coefficient sets are
bit-identical and none of 629,050 is unstable. Typical notches differ by
under 0.001 dB at the centre and 0.004 dB within 8% of it. Extreme ones (5
Hz, 60 dB, Q 20) differ by up to 3.4 dB at the exact centre, where float
coefficients already put libm up to 37.8 dB from the exact design. The
Cody-Waite and Sterbenz citations were confirmed from secondary sources,
not from the books.

Why this shows on the RP2350. `sinf`, `cosf` and both kernels are in SRAM,
but `__ieee754_rem_pio2f` was left in flash because it "did not profile",
and it only runs for centres above 400 Hz. Three firmwares on alpha2
`031f2549`, nine boots flashed A R F F R A A R F, on section 14's setup. A
is unchanged, R moves only `lib_a-ef_rem_pio2.o` to SRAM, F is the helper:

| Mean of three boots (range) | A | R | F |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core1 busy, PC samples | 67.04% (66.93-67.14) | 64.79% (64.77-64.81) | 59.71% (59.60-59.86) |
| `sinf`, `cosf` and helpers | 10.51% | 7.72% | 0 |
| Core1 in flash | 5.83% | 1.70% | 1.90% |
| `RPLD.C1` | 65.00% | 62.40% | 57.02% |
| Worst armed rate-loop gap | 0.91 ms | 0.88 ms | 0.86 ms |

So about 2.3 of the 7.3 points are the flash penalty and the other 5 are
arithmetic: 48 notches at half the 3201 Hz gyro rate is about 76,800
recalculations a second, at roughly 270 cycles each for the two trig calls
(derived from the settings and the 10.5% share). STM32 was not measured.
Each log again dropped messages at its start, 75-213 here, in all three
builds.

### Superseded 2026-11-23 by the review of `c8441d894f`

The gain bounds above (0.001 dB and 0.004 dB typical, 3.4 dB and 0.017 dB
extreme) are wrong, and the same text is in that commit's comment and
message. The sweep used round frequencies and offsets of 2-8%, so it missed
the null. They are left here because they are what the commit claims.

Three million random float cases per range, ChibiOS arithmetic
(`-fsingle-precision-constant`), host glibc as libm, not newlib
(`tools/notch_sincos/review_reconcile/`; extract `helper.inc` from the
commit):

| Range | Bit-identical | Helper against libm, centre / within 8% | Centre error against exact design, p99.9, libm / helper | Largest null error, both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical: 1-8 kHz, 30 Hz-0.45 fs, 15-40 dB, Q 1-11.7 | 88.0% | 0.04 / 0.20 dB | 0.0156 / 0.0156 dB | 0.005 Hz |
| High Q: 1-8 kHz, 5-200 Hz, 15-60 dB, Q 20-100 | 97.9% | 41.50 / 27.99 dB | 43.49 / 43.49 dB | 0.019 Hz |
| Extreme: 0.4-8 kHz, 5 Hz-0.49 fs, 15-60 dB, Q 1-20 | 91.4% | 11.75 / 5.18 dB | 23.56 / 23.56 dB | 0.022 Hz |

No case was unstable. A one-ulp change in cosine moves a deep, narrow null
by a few mHz, so its depth at one exact frequency is a lottery for both.

Review finding rejected: "the helper's cosine error loses rejection on
high-Q notches". It is real for single inputs and goes both ways, and the
helper is further from the exact design in 1.0% of high-Q cases and closer
in 1.0%. At 64.94 Hz, Q 98.7, libm gives -49.67 dB and the helper -45.85.
At 5.87 Hz, Q 11.1, libm gives -39.13 and the helper -46.29. The reported
Q 199.7 case needs more than the parameter ranges allow (about 495/5), and
is identical in ChibiOS arithmetic (-49.05 dB).

Review findings accepted, not yet fixed:

- `CoefficientsTest` cannot see cosine error at low frequency: at 5 Hz, 8
  kHz, cos + 1e-5 moves the three outputs by at most 3.9e-8 against a 3e-6
  tolerance, with a pole radius of 1.00171.
- The 7.7e-8 step is not at the branch change, where it is 1.75e-8 at pi/4
  and 1.02e-8 at 3pi/4. The largest errors (6.96e-8 sine, 7.77e-8 cosine)
  are 1.17 and 1.30 ulp; the 1.38 and 1.48 ulp maxima are at other angles.
- Forced inline is not needed on master, whose RAM placement is by object
  file, and costs 204 B on H743. The RP2350 registry is per function.
- Timing exists only for the RP2350, which master does not have.
